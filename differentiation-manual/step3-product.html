Product: Breaking Free from "Write an Essay"

Product differentiation offers diverse ways for students to demonstrate mastery. It's about expanding beyond the default "written response" and recognising that students can show deep learning through multiple formats.

The whole education system has become heavily reliant on essays and traditional written formats. Product differentiation says: What if students could create posters, build websites, design presentations, code something, record podcasts, create infographics?

AI makes it possible to support students in producing a much wider range of products—which means more students can show what they know in ways that work for them.

Product Differentiation Strategies

2

Choice Boards & Menus

▼

Grids or menus of activity options that allow students to select how they will demonstrate learning. Students choose from multiple product options, all of which address the same learning goals but offer different ways to show mastery.

When to use: When you want to give students agency in how they demonstrate learning. Choice boards work well for culminating projects, application tasks, or ongoing skill practice. They increase engagement by honoring student preferences.
Example in practice: A 3x3 choice board for showing understanding of ecosystems. Students pick 3 (one from each row):
  • Row 1 (Visual): Create diagram | Design infographic | Build model
  • Row 2 (Written): Write article | Create field guide | Compose poem
  • Row 3 (Performance): Present to class | Record video | Create interactive quiz

AI Prompts:

Create a Choice Board

Design a choice board for [topic/unit]. My KUDs are: [paste KUDs]

Create a 3x3 grid with 9 different product options that:

  • All address the same learning goals but through different formats
  • Vary in modality (visual, written, oral, digital, hands-on)
  • Offer different entry points for different learning preferences
  • Are equally rigorous (not "hard" vs "easy" options)

Students should choose [number] options to complete. For each option, provide a brief description and what the final product should include.

Create a Tiered Choice Board

Design a choice board with built-in differentiation by readiness. Create three versions:

  • Board A (Support): More structured options with clear steps and scaffolds
  • Board B (Core): Standard challenge level
  • Board C (Extension): More open-ended, requires synthesis or evaluation

All boards should offer similar types of products (visual, written, performance) but at different complexity levels. Topic: [topic], KUDs: [paste KUDs]

Create Product-Specific Guidance

A student has chosen to [specific product from choice board, e.g., "create an infographic" or "record a podcast episode"].

Create step-by-step guidance for this student that includes:

  • What elements the product must include to demonstrate learning
  • Steps to create it (considering they may not have done this before)
  • Tools or resources they could use
  • How they'll be assessed (what "quality" looks like)

RAFT (Role, Audience, Format, Topic)

▼

A technique where students demonstrate learning by taking on a specific Role, writing for a targeted Audience, in a chosen Format, about a specified Topic. RAFT makes learning feel more authentic and engaging.

When to use: When you want students to apply content knowledge in creative, authentic ways. RAFT works especially well for content that can be viewed from multiple perspectives or communicated to different audiences.
Example in practice: Teaching about the water cycle:
  • Role: Water droplet
  • Audience: Elementary students
  • Format: Travel diary
  • Topic: Journey through the water cycle
Student writes a first-person diary from the water droplet's perspective, explaining the cycle in accessible language.

AI Prompts:

Generate RAFT Options

Create 6-8 RAFT options for demonstrating understanding of [topic]. My KUDs are: [paste KUDs]

For each option, specify:

  • Role: Who the student is writing as
  • Audience: Who they're writing to
  • Format: What type of writing/product
  • Topic: What specific aspect they're addressing

Make the options varied and creative, but ensure each would genuinely demonstrate the KUDs.

Differentiate RAFT by Readiness

Topic: [topic]. Create RAFT options at three levels:

  • Level 1: Clear role/audience/format with concrete topic
  • Level 2: Standard complexity
  • Level 3: More sophisticated role/audience requiring abstract thinking

Example for Level 3: Role might be "historian analysing primary sources," Audience might be "academic journal readers," Format might be "scholarly article."

Support Students in Completing RAFT

A student is working on this RAFT:

  • Role: [role]
  • Audience: [audience]
  • Format: [format]
  • Topic: [topic]

Provide:

  • Guidance on how to write from this role's perspective
  • What this audience needs to know/what tone to use
  • Structural elements this format typically includes
  • 3-4 sentence starters to help them begin

Negotiated Criteria

▼

Collaborate with students to determine the core requirements for a product while allowing them to add personal quality goals. Students have voice in what "excellent work" looks like while ensuring essential elements are present.

When to use: When you want to build student ownership and agency while maintaining rigor. Works well for projects where students have choice in format but you need to ensure certain learning goals are demonstrated.
Example in practice: For a research project, teacher establishes non-negotiables (must include 3 credible sources, must address essential question, must show evidence of analysis). Students then add their own quality goals (e.g., "I want to include original graphics" or "I want to interview an expert").

AI Prompts:

Establish Non-Negotiable Criteria

Students are creating [type of product] to demonstrate [KUDs]. What are the non-negotiable criteria—the elements that MUST be present for students to demonstrate the learning goals?

Create a list of 5-7 essential criteria that:

  • Directly connect to the KUDs
  • Are observable/assessable
  • Apply regardless of the specific approach students take

These are the baseline requirements all students must meet.

Generate Student Choice Criteria Options

Beyond the non-negotiable criteria, what additional quality goals could students choose to pursue? Generate 8-10 options that would enhance their [product type], such as:

  • Visual design elements
  • Additional depth of research
  • Creative presentation methods
  • Audience engagement strategies

Students should select 2-3 of these to pursue, making their work feel personalised while meeting core requirements.

Tiered Assessments

▼

Provide multiple versions of a summative task that assess the same standards but vary in complexity, scaffolding, or mode of expression. All students demonstrate the same learning goals, but the pathway to demonstrating them differs.

When to use: When students have significantly varied readiness levels and you want to assess their learning accurately without ceiling or floor effects. Tiered assessments ensure you see what students actually know, not what they can't access due to task design.
Example in practice: All students demonstrate understanding of persuasive writing:
  • Tier 1: Write persuasive paragraph using provided structure and sentence starters
  • Tier 2: Write persuasive essay with standard requirements
  • Tier 3: Write persuasive essay addressing counterarguments and evaluating evidence quality

AI Prompts:

Create Tiered Assessment Versions

Create three versions of an assessment for [topic]. All versions must assess the same KUDs: [paste KUDs]

  • Version A (Support): More scaffolding, step-by-step guidance, concrete application
  • Version B (Core): Standard expectations for grade level
  • Version C (Extension): More complex application, requires synthesis/evaluation

Important: All three versions should be equally rigorous in demonstrating the KUDs—vary the support and complexity, not the learning goals.

Tier by Changing Product Format

Create three assessment options that address the same KUDs but through different product formats:

  • Option 1: Traditional written format
  • Option 2: Visual/graphic format
  • Option 3: Oral/presentation format

Ensure all three options would provide equal evidence of student learning. Topic: [topic], KUDs: [paste KUDs]

Expanding the Product Repertoire with AI

▼

AI tools make it possible to support students in creating products that were previously too complex or time-consuming to scaffold. Consider these possibilities:

Digital products students can now create with AI support:
  • Websites: Simple HTML sites to present research or projects
  • Podcasts/Audio: Scripted episodes explaining concepts
  • Infographics: Visual data representation
  • Interactive presentations: Beyond basic slideshows
  • Code projects: Simple programs demonstrating concepts
  • Digital posters: Combining text, images, data
  • Video content: Explainer videos, documentaries, tutorials

AI Prompts:

Support Non-Traditional Products

A student wants to demonstrate learning about [topic] by creating [specific product type, e.g., "a podcast episode" or "a simple website"].

This is new territory for them. Provide:

  • A simple template or structure for this product type
  • Step-by-step guidance for creation
  • What content elements must be included to demonstrate the KUDs: [paste KUDs]
  • Tools they could use (free/accessible options)
  • How to organise their work before creating the product

Generate Product-Specific Rubrics

Create an assessment rubric for [specific product type] that students are creating to demonstrate [KUDs].

The rubric should include criteria for:

  • Content accuracy and completeness
  • Demonstration of the specific KUDs
  • Quality specific to this product format (e.g., for podcast: audio clarity, engagement, pacing)
  • Organisation and presentation

Use 4-point scale: Emerging, Developing, Proficient, Exemplary

Transform Traditional to Digital Product

I traditionally have students [describe traditional assignment, e.g., "write a research paper" or "create a poster"].

Suggest 3-4 digital product alternatives that:

  • Would demonstrate the same learning (KUDs: [paste KUDs])
  • Engage different skills or modalities
  • Are feasible for [grade level] students with AI support
  • Maintain or increase rigor

For each alternative, explain what makes it equivalent to the original assignment in terms of demonstrating learning.

Performance & Presentation Products

▼

Some students demonstrate their learning best through performance—presenting, demonstrating, teaching, or performing for an audience.

When to use: When learning involves demonstrable skills, when public speaking is a goal, or when students excel at oral communication over written.
Examples: Teach-backs, demonstrations, debates, presentations, gallery walks, simulations

AI Prompts:

Design a Performance Task

Students need to demonstrate [KUDs] through a performance or presentation. Design a task where students [specify: teach, demonstrate, present, debate] to show mastery. Provide task description, success criteria, preparation guidance, and assessment rubric.

13

Creative & Artistic Products

▼

Creative products allow students to demonstrate understanding through art, design, storytelling, or creative expression.

When to use: When content has emotional, thematic, or interpretive dimensions; when students need alternative ways to show understanding.
Examples: Visual art, comics/graphic novels, music/songs, poetry, sculptures/3D models, dramatic scripts

AI Prompts:

Design Creative Product Options

Create 4-5 creative product options for demonstrating [KUDs]. For each: explain the form, required content, how creativity enhances learning, and assessment criteria. Ensure artistic skill doesn't become a barrier.

15

Collaborative Group Products

▼

Some learning is best demonstrated through collaboration—products that require students to work together and create something no individual could create alone.

When to use: When learning goals include collaboration skills, when tasks benefit from multiple perspectives, or when product scope requires distributed effort.
Examples: Group research projects, collaborative websites/wikis, documentary/video projects, problem-solving challenges

AI Prompts:

Design Collaborative Product

Design a group product for [KUDs] where students work in groups of [number]. Ensure genuine collaboration, identifiable individual contributions, and assessment of both group product and individual learning. Include task description, roles, timeline, criteria.
