Establish the Learning Destination

Know, Understand, Do (KUDs)

The Foundation of All Differentiation

Before you differentiate anything, you must have rock-solid clarity about what students should end up with at the conclusion of the lesson. Without this clarity, differentiation becomes arbitrary.

The essential goals (KUDs) typically remain constant for all students; what varies is how they access them, process them, and demonstrate them.

Why This Step Matters

Carol Ann Tomlinson emphasises that clarity about learning goals is the "non-negotiable" foundation of differentiation. When you're clear about where students are going, you can:

  • Differentiate how students get there (content, process, product)
  • Maintain high expectations for all students
  • Assess whether differentiation is actually working
  • Communicate goals clearly to students and parents

"When we are clear about what matters most, we can let go of what matters less."
- Carol Ann Tomlinson

The KUD Framework

K What students must KNOW

The essential facts, vocabulary, definitions, and information.

Examples:
  • Key vocabulary terms and their definitions
  • Important dates, names, or formulas
  • Factual information that forms the knowledge base

U What students must UNDERSTAND

The key concepts, principles, and generalizations that transfer beyond this specific lesson.

Examples:
  • "Authors use metaphor to create deeper meaning"
  • "Climate influences cultural development"
  • "Patterns in mathematics help us predict and problem-solve"
💡 Tip: Understandings are often written as complete sentences that begin with phrases like "Students will understand that..."

D What students must be able to DO

The specific skills students should be able to demonstrate.

Examples:
  • Analyse a primary source document
  • Solve multi-step equations
  • Write a persuasive paragraph with evidence
  • Create a line graph from data

💡 Remember: Context is Key

For AI prompts to work at their best, provide context about your students and teaching situation. Include details like:

Year level, student ages, class size, prior learning, learning styles present in your class, specific needs or challenges, your curriculum requirements.

The more context you provide, the more tailored and useful the AI's suggestions will be.

Clarify KUDs from Existing Materials

▼

I'm teaching [topic/unit]. Here's my current lesson plan: [paste plan].

Help me identify the essential Know, Understand, and Do for this lesson. Format your response as:

  • KNOW: [factual knowledge]
  • UNDERSTAND: [key concepts as complete sentences]
  • DO: [specific skills]

Then ask me clarifying questions if any goals seem unclear or too broad.

Test Your KUDs for Clarity

▼

Here are my KUDs for this lesson:

KNOW: [your list]
UNDERSTAND: [your list]
DO: [your list]

Evaluate these goals:

  • Are they specific and measurable?
  • Is there a clear distinction between facts (Know), concepts (Understand), and skills (Do)?
  • Are the Understandings transferable beyond this specific lesson?
  • Are there any "nice to know" elements that aren't actually essential?

Provide feedback and suggest refinements.

Build KUDs from Scratch

▼

I'm planning a lesson on [topic] for [grade level] students. The curriculum outcomes I need to address are: [paste outcomes].

Help me develop clear Know, Understand, and Do goals for this lesson. Make sure:

  • The KNOW elements are concrete and factual
  • The UNDERSTAND elements are conceptual and transferable
  • The DO elements are specific, observable skills

Ask me questions about student context or prior knowledge if needed.

Stress-Test Your Goals (Anticipatory Differentiation)

▼

Here are my KUDs: [paste your KUDs]

Act as a critical friend and stress-test these goals:

  • What might students misunderstand based on how these are worded?
  • Are there gaps where students might lack prerequisite knowledge?
  • Are any of these goals too ambitious or too limited for the lesson timeframe?
  • What would a struggling student need to access these goals? What would an advanced student need to be challenged by them?
